Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Rotatable Networks: An
Invariance Model for Weighted Graphs, with Spectral Sufficiency, an
Exact Conditional Test, and Heavy-Tailed Extensions," for publication in
the Journal of the American Statistical Association, Theory and Methods.

The problem the paper solves in one sentence: practitioners across
network neuroscience, genomics, and finance model weighted networks as
low-rank signal plus rotation-invariant noise and interpret scalar
summaries against informal nulls, and no existing framework says when
that model class is forced, which summaries carry information beyond the
eigenvalues, or how to test the symmetry exactly at finite n.

The result we would like a referee to remember: under joint rotatability
with sampling consistency, the model class is exactly the mixtures of
spiked Gaussian orthogonal ensembles, the observed spectrum is
sufficient with a known Haar conditional, and therefore every network
statistic admits an exact finite-sample conditional test and a
conditional residual, with no parameters estimated and calibration for
any observed spectrum.
